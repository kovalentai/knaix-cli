Revenue Recognition Policy — Software and Subscription Arrangements

1. Framework. The company recognizes revenue in accordance with the five-step model: identify the contract, identify the performance obligations, determine the transaction price, allocate the price to the obligations, and recognize revenue as each obligation is satisfied.

2. Subscription Revenue. Revenue from term-based software subscriptions is recognized ratably over the subscription period, beginning on the date access to the software is made available to the customer, not the date the contract is signed.

3. Implementation Services. One-time implementation and onboarding fees are recognized as the services are performed, measured by the proportion of labor hours incurred to date relative to total estimated hours, provided the services are distinct from the subscription.

4. Deferred Revenue. Amounts invoiced in advance of delivery are recorded as deferred revenue, a contract liability, and released to revenue as the related performance obligation is satisfied. Deferred revenue expected to be recognized beyond twelve months is classified as long-term.

5. Variable Consideration. Usage-based fees and performance bonuses are estimated using the expected-value method and included in the transaction price only to the extent it is probable that a significant reversal of cumulative revenue will not occur.

6. Standalone Selling Price. When a contract bundles multiple obligations, the transaction price is allocated based on the relative standalone selling price of each obligation, estimated using observable prices where available and the adjusted market-assessment approach otherwise.

7. Contract Modifications. A contract modification that adds distinct goods or services at their standalone selling price is accounted for as a separate contract. Otherwise, the modification is accounted for prospectively over the remaining performance obligations.

8. Refunds and Credits. A refund liability is recognized for consideration the company expects to refund to customers, measured at each reporting date. Service credits issued for missed service levels reduce revenue in the period the credit is granted.

9. Contract Costs. Incremental costs of obtaining a contract, such as sales commissions, are capitalized and amortized over the expected period of benefit, which the company estimates as the average customer life of thirty-six (36) months, unless the amortization period is one year or less.

10. Significant Financing. When the timing of payments provides the customer or the company with a significant financing benefit exceeding twelve months, the transaction price is adjusted for the time value of money using a discount rate that reflects the customer's credit characteristics.
