Clinical Trial Protocol — Phase II Study of Compound NX-114

1. Study Design. This is a randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled Phase II study evaluating the efficacy and safety of NX-114 in adults with moderate-to-severe plaque psoriasis. Participants are randomized in a 2:1 ratio to receive NX-114 or matching placebo over a twelve-week treatment period.

2. Primary Endpoint. The primary efficacy endpoint is the proportion of participants achieving at least a 75% reduction in the Psoriasis Area and Severity Index (PASI 75) at Week 12 compared with baseline.

3. Inclusion Criteria. Eligible participants are 18 to 75 years of age, have a diagnosis of plaque psoriasis for at least six months, and have a baseline PASI score of 12 or greater with at least 10% body-surface-area involvement.

4. Exclusion Criteria. Participants are excluded if they have received any biologic therapy within the twelve weeks before screening, have a history of active tuberculosis, or are pregnant or breastfeeding. Guttate, erythrodermic, and pustular psoriasis are excluded.

5. Dosing. NX-114 is administered as a 150 mg subcutaneous injection once every two weeks. The first dose is given under direct clinical supervision, and participants are observed for at least thirty (30) minutes after each of the first two injections for signs of hypersensitivity.

6. Adverse Event Reporting. All serious adverse events must be reported by the investigator to the sponsor within twenty-four (24) hours of the site becoming aware of the event. The report is followed by a written narrative within five (5) calendar days.

7. Informed Consent. Written informed consent must be obtained from each participant before any study-specific procedure is performed. The consent process must be documented, and the participant must receive a signed copy of the consent form.

8. Randomization and Blinding. Randomization is managed centrally through an interactive web response system. The blind may be broken only in a medical emergency where knowledge of the treatment assignment is essential to the participant's care.

9. Rescue Medication. Topical corticosteroids of low to medium potency are permitted as rescue medication after Week 4 but must be recorded. Systemic therapies and phototherapy are prohibited for the duration of the treatment period.

10. Withdrawal Criteria. A participant is withdrawn from the study if they experience a life-threatening adverse reaction, become pregnant, or require a prohibited systemic therapy. Withdrawn participants are asked to complete an early-termination visit including safety assessments.
